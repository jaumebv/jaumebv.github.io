Overview

Inspired by games like Rain World, Procedural Salamander AI was built in C++ using Raylib by a three-person team to explore how procedural animation and reactive AI can produce lifelike creature movement without a single keyframed sprite.

Every stride, spine bend, tail whip and tongue flick emerges live from inverse kinematics, spring-mass chains and autonomous predator–prey perception—accompanied by real-time ImGui debug visualisers for joints, vision cones and targets.

Key systems

Motion from math & rules.

INVERSE KINEMATICS

2D Inverse Kinematics for Limbs

Custom 2D IK solvers compute joint angles dynamically for all four legs without predefined animation curves.

  • Adaptive foot placement: Feet step smoothly toward anchor targets as the spine advances while avoiding joint flips or intersections.
  • Debug-friendly: Live joint overlays visualise target anchors, segment constraints and limb reach in real time.
2D IKProcedural gait
SPRING-MASS PHYSICS

Procedural Tail & Body Dynamics

A spring-mass physics chain drives the salamander’s segmented spine and flexible tail, producing fluid, snake-like motion that reacts physically to turns and impulses.

  • Distance & angular constraints: Preserves body volume while letting waves propagate naturally down the tail.
  • Force-reactive motion: Sharp directional turns whip the tail proportionally to velocity and curvature.
Spring-mass chainC++ · Raylib
EMERGENT AI

Reactive Predator–Prey Fly Behaviour

Autonomous flies wander with steering behaviours and scatter whenever they detect the salamander’s approach, triggering spontaneous hunting chases and tongue strikes.

  • Perception cones & target selection: The salamander tracks visible prey within its field of view and prioritises the closest fly.
  • Emergent chases: Simple local rules produce unpredictable, organic hunting arcs every run.
